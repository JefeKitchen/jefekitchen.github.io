GUIDE
Method Oven
Temp 350°F

⚠️

Per GE's official instructions for the GGS600AVFS. Re-seasoning uses the oven, not the stovetop burner. This is different from the initial seasoning process.

What You Need
  1. —
    Avocado oil — high smoke point, best choice for seasoning
  2. —
    Paper towels — for applying and wiping oil
  3. —
    Hot water and a sponge — for initial clean
  4. —
    Steel wool — only if there is rust to remove first
· · · ·
Step 1 — Clean the Surface
  1. 1
    If there is any rust, remove it now with steel wool. This is the only time steel wool is acceptable on the griddle.
  2. 2
    Scrub the surface with hot water and a sponge to remove as much of the old seasoning as possible. You are starting fresh.
  3. 3
    Towel dry completely. Do not let it air dry — pooled water causes rust. Make sure every part of the surface is dry before moving on.
· · · ·
Step 2 — Apply the Oil
  1. 1
    Apply a very thin layer of avocado oil across the entire surface using a paper towel. Cover every part — top, sides, and edges.
  2. 2
    Wipe off the excess with a fresh paper towel. The surface should look almost dry. Too much oil gets gummy instead of bonding to the metal. Thin is the whole game.

Why So Thin?

A thick layer of oil won't polymerize — it just sits on the surface and goes sticky. The thinner the coat, the better it bonds. You want it to look like you barely applied anything.

· · · ·
Step 3 — Oven Season
  1. 1
    Preheat your oven to 350°F.
  2. 2
    Place the griddle in the oven and bake for 1 hour. It may smoke a little — this is normal. That's the oil polymerizing and bonding to the cast iron.
  3. 3
    Turn the oven off and let the griddle cool inside the oven. Do not rush this. Let it come down to room temperature on its own.
  4. 4
    Once cool, repeat the oil and oven process 2–3 more times. Each round builds another layer of seasoning. The more rounds, the better the surface.

How Many Rounds?

GE says repeat as necessary. For a full re-season from scratch, 3–4 rounds is ideal. The surface should darken and develop a slight sheen by the end.

After Every Cook — Keep It Seasoned

  • While still warm, scrape off any food debris
  • Rinse with hot water and scrub with a sponge
  • Towel dry immediately — never air dry
  • Apply a very thin layer of oil before it cools
  • Never use soap, steel wool, or a dishwasher
  • Never let water pool or sit on the surface
