GUIDE
Method Stovetop
Rounds 3–4
Oil Avocado
🔥

Do this before cooking anything on the griddle. Initial seasoning uses the stovetop burner, not the oven. This is a one-time process.

What You Need
  1. —
    Avocado oil — high smoke point, best choice for seasoning
  2. —
    Paper towels or a heavy cloth — for applying and wiping oil
  3. —
    Warm soapy water — for the initial wash
  4. —
    1 quart water + 1 cup white vinegar — for the rinse
· · · ·
Step 1 — First Time Wash
  1. 1
    Wash the griddle with warm soapy water to remove any factory oils or protective coating. This is the only time you will ever use soap on it.
  2. 2
    Rinse with a mixture of 1 quart water and 1 cup white vinegar.
  3. 3
    Dry completely with a towel. Do not air dry — moisture causes rust.
· · · ·
Step 2 — Burn Off the Factory Coating
  1. 1
    Place the griddle over the center oval burner and turn the heat to high.
  2. 2
    Let it heat for 10–15 minutes until the surface starts to discolor and may smoke. That's the factory coating burning off. This is normal.
  3. 3
    Drop heat to medium and let it settle before the first oil round.
· · · ·
Step 3 — Season (Repeat 3–4 Times)
  1. 1
    Put about 1 teaspoon of avocado oil on the surface. Spread it thin across the entire surface with a paper towel or heavy cloth. It should look almost dry — thin is the whole game.
  2. 2
    Keep the burner on medium. The oil will start to smoke after a minute or two — that means it's bonding to the surface. Wait until it stops smoking completely.
  3. 3
    Let it cool down a bit — not all the way, just enough that it's no longer scorching. Then repeat the oil and heat process. Do this 3–4 rounds total.

How Do I Know It's Working?

The surface will darken with each round and develop a slight sheen by the end. Uneven patches are normal on the first seasoning — they fill in with use.

· · · ·
Step 4 — Final Coat
  1. 1
    After the last round, apply one final very thin coat of avocado oil while it's still warm. This protects the surface until you cook on it.
  2. 2
    Let it cool completely. You're done. It's ready to cook on.

After Every Cook — Keep It Seasoned

  • While still warm, scrape off any food debris
  • Splash a little water to steam clean, scrape again
  • Wipe down with a paper towel
  • Apply a very thin layer of oil before it cools
  • Never use soap, steel wool, or a dishwasher
  • Never let water pool or sit on the surface
